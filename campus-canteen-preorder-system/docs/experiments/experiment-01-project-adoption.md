## Objective and adoption rationale

Adopt one real-world software project and identify its problem, stakeholders, and justification. The supplied lab record states that students wait in long queues during short breaks and lunch. The proposed system lets students browse the menu, place advance orders, and follow readiness status; canteen staff manage incoming orders and item availability.

## Problem statement

Students frequently face long queues and delays while purchasing food from the college canteen, especially during short breaks and peak hours. Canteen staff also face difficulty managing multiple orders, maintaining order sequence, and handling rush periods. Students generally need to visit the counter, wait, place an order, and then wait for preparation. This consumes break time and can cause crowding and order confusion. The proposed Campus Canteen Pre-Order System provides a digital menu, advance ordering of available items, and an order-ready status. Canteen staff can view and update incoming orders.

## Stakeholders and success measures

| Stakeholder | Interest | Initial success measure to validate |
|---|---|---|
| Students | Find available food and collect orders | Shorter counter wait; clear order status |
| Canteen staff | Receive, prepare, and hand over orders | Fewer missed or confused orders |
| Canteen manager | Monitor queue and operations | Menu and daily queue visible in one place |
| System administrator | Maintain accounts and system | Role controls and recoverable local database |

## Software-crisis risks identified

- Requirements may change when students and canteen staff review the workflow.
- Missing validation could cause an incorrect quantity, price, or order state.
- A poorly sized schedule could leave integration and acceptance testing too late.
- Weak module boundaries could make menu and status changes expensive to maintain.
- Account and session flaws could expose student order data or staff controls.
